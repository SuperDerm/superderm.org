Describe What You See

The terms annotators use for visible skin findings. Pick the ones that fit; mark uncertainty rather than guessing. This vocabulary is a draft awaiting clinical review.

Quick Check

Five definitions, four choices each. Takes about a minute.

Primary Lesions

Macule
Flat, non-palpable change in colour, small (commonly under 1 cm).
Patch
Flat, non-palpable change in colour, larger (commonly 1 cm or more).
Papule
Raised, palpable lesion, small (commonly under 1 cm).
Plaque
Raised, plateau-like lesion, larger (commonly 1 cm or more).
Nodule
Raised or deep, rounded lesion with depth, commonly 1 cm or more.
Vesicle
Small fluid-filled blister.
Bulla
Large fluid-filled blister.
Pustule
Visible collection of pus.
Wheal
Transient, oedematous papule or plaque.
Cyst
Enclosed cavity with liquid or semi-solid contents.

Secondary Changes

Scale
Visible flakes of stratum corneum.
Crust
Dried serum, blood or pus on the surface.
Erosion
Superficial loss of epidermis.
Ulcer
Deeper loss of epidermis and dermis.
Fissure
Linear crack in the skin.
Excoriation
Scratch mark or picked area.
Lichenification
Thickened skin with accentuated skin markings.
Atrophy
Thinned skin.
Scar
Fibrous tissue replacing normal skin.
Eschar or necrosis
Dead tissue, often black or dark crust adherent to the base.

Vascular and Purpuric

Petechiae
Pinpoint non-blanching purpura.
Purpura
Non-blanching discoloration from blood in the skin.
Telangiectasia
Visible small dilated blood vessels.

Arrangement and Shape

Annular
Ring-shaped, often with central clearing.
Grouped or clustered
Lesions gathered together in a group.
Linear
Arranged in a line.
Umbilicated
Central depression in a raised lesion.
