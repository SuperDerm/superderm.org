22:T1077,<h1>Image Credits</h1>
<p>SuperDerm shows two kinds of photographs: clinical photographs from a public dataset whose licence permits public display, and synthetic illustrations used for practice and testing. No photograph from the team&#39;s own cases appears on the site.</p>
<h2 id="photographs-from-the-pad-ufes-20-public-dataset">Photographs from the PAD-UFES-20 Public Dataset<a class="heading-anchor" href="#photographs-from-the-pad-ufes-20-public-dataset" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2>
<p>The photograph on the homepage, the example on <a href="/annotate">Annotate photos</a>, the <a href="/learn/cases">teaching cases</a> and the preview annotation queue use clinical close-up photographs from <strong>PAD-UFES-20</strong>, a dataset of skin lesions photographed with smartphones at a dermatology service in Brazil.</p>
<ul>
<li><strong>Creators:</strong> Pacheco AGC, Lima GR, Salomão AS, et al. PAD-UFES-20: A skin lesion dataset composed of patient data and clinical images collected from smartphones. <em>Data in Brief.</em> 2020;32:106221. <a href="https://doi.org/10.1016/j.dib.2020.106221">doi:10.1016/j.dib.2020.106221</a></li>
<li><strong>Dataset:</strong> <a href="https://data.mendeley.com/datasets/zr7vgbcyr2/1">Mendeley Data, doi:10.17632/zr7vgbcyr2.1</a></li>
<li><strong>Licence:</strong> <a href="https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/">Creative Commons Attribution 4.0 International (CC BY 4.0)</a>.</li>
<li><strong>Changes:</strong> converted to WebP or JPEG with metadata removed; one image resized. Outlines and boxes are drawn by the site on a separate layer; the photographs themselves are not edited.</li>
<li><strong>Diagnoses and histories:</strong> the teaching cases show the values recorded in the dataset. Where the dataset marks a lesion as biopsied, the diagnosis is the biopsy result; otherwise it is the clinical diagnosis of the dermatologists who collected the data.</li>
</ul>

<h2 id="synthetic-photographs">Synthetic Photographs<a class="heading-anchor" href="#synthetic-photographs" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2>
<p>The practice tasks and the automated tests use synthetic photographs.</p>
<ul>
<li>They are generated by a script from a fixed seed: procedural images of a skin-toned background with schematic lesion shapes.</li>
<li>They depict no patient and no real disease, and each is stamped &quot;SYNTHETIC · NOT A PATIENT&quot;.</li>
<li>They are released under CC0 1.0. In the platform they are recorded as the source &quot;Synthetic preview photographs&quot;.</li>
</ul>
<p>You may reuse the synthetic photographs freely. They illustrate the workflows; do not present them as clinical images.</p>
