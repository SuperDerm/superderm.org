1f:T1f4e,<h1>Contribute a Case</h1>
<p>Use <a href="/contribute">Contribute a case</a> to share one case with one or more clinical photographs. Rare conditions are especially valuable, and so are the common conditions that resemble them. The disease can be unknown: authorized photographs can still be annotated for visible findings.</p>
<p>This form is for research contributions. It does not give medical advice.</p>
<h2 id="who-can-contribute">Who Can Contribute<a class="heading-anchor" href="#who-can-contribute" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2>
<p>Clinical teams, dataset authors, and patients or their authorized representatives. Everyone uses the same restricted intake. You must be signed in and have accepted the community rules; see <a href="/docs/getting-started/overview">Getting started</a>.</p>
<h2 id="before-you-start">Before You Start<a class="heading-anchor" href="#before-you-start" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2>
<ul>
<li>Photos must be clinical photographs from a phone or camera, in JPEG or PNG, up to 25 MiB and 80 million pixels each. Export HEIC/HEIF photos as JPEG first. Dermoscopic and histopathology images do not belong here.</li>
<li>Leave names, dates of birth, record numbers and other identifiers out of every text field.</li>
<li>Know which uses your consent or agreement covers.</li>
</ul>

<p><strong>Contribute a Case</strong> only preselects the rare category. The clinical lead verifies rare-disease status.</p>
<h2 id="case-description">Case Description<a class="heading-anchor" href="#case-description" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2>
<p>All optional: a brief history (onset, duration, symptoms, relevant context), an age band, the body site, and conditions it resembles. The last helps match rare conditions with their common mimics.</p>

<td>Required when you give a diagnosis: Unknown, Pathology, Laboratory testing, Genetic testing, Clinical records, Published case, Other source record.</td>

<p>Each item may be unknown; choose <strong>Unknown</strong> rather than guessing. A diagnosis without supporting evidence is recorded as reported, pending verification by a clinical reviewer. Do not attach supporting documents: the restricted evidence workflow for them is Planned.</p>
<h2 id="sharing-permissions">Sharing Permissions<a class="heading-anchor" href="#sharing-permissions" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2>
<p>Tick only the uses your consent or agreement covers. Unknown uses stay closed. The first two start ticked.</p>

<p>Also required: the <strong>basis for these permissions</strong> (for example, written patient consent for research use and publication, with the form version) and the <strong>source of the photographs</strong> (for example, your clinic, or a published case report with its citation). See <a href="/docs/data/access-and-withdrawal">Data access and withdrawal</a> for how uses are enforced.</p>
<h2 id="photographs">Photographs<a class="heading-anchor" href="#photographs" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2>
<p>Add one or more photos. Each upload goes to private staging, and the platform checks that the full file arrived. The thumbnails you see exist only in your browser. A data steward reviews photos for identifying details before any use.</p>
<h2 id="save-preview-and-submit">Save, Preview and Submit<a class="heading-anchor" href="#save-preview-and-submit" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2>
<ol>
<li>Select <strong>Save Draft</strong> at any time. The first save creates your submission ID: &quot;Draft saved · SUB-…&quot;.</li>
<li>Select <strong>Preview</strong>. Missing or invalid fields are listed at the top, each linked to its field.</li>
<li>Check the preview, then select <strong>Submit for Review</strong>, or <strong>Back to Edit</strong>.</li>
</ol>
<p>The browser warns you before you leave with unsaved changes. To continue a saved draft later, open <strong>Contributions</strong> in <a href="/contribute">My workspace</a> and select <strong>Resume Draft</strong>. The form reopens with your saved answers and shows &quot;Draft SUB-… reopened&quot;, and the same submission ID continues through to the receipt. Only you can reopen your draft, and a submitted revision cannot be reopened.</p>
<h2 id="your-receipt">Your Receipt<a class="heading-anchor" href="#your-receipt" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2>
<p>After submitting you see <strong>Received for Review</strong> with the submission ID (<code>SUB-</code> and ten characters), the status <strong>Pending Review</strong> and the revision number. Keep the ID: it stays the same if a reviewer asks for more information and you send a revision. You also get a notification, and the case appears under <strong>Contributions</strong> in <a href="/contribute">My workspace</a>. A submitted revision is locked.</p>
<h2 id="what-happens-next">What Happens Next<a class="heading-anchor" href="#what-happens-next" aria-label="Link to this section">#</a></h2>
<p>A contribution can be Draft, Pending review, In review, More information required, Accepted, Partially accepted, Declined or Withdrawn.</p>
<p>Reviewer tools for contributions are Planned. Until they are available, a submitted case stays in <strong>Pending Review</strong>, and its photos stay in private staging. When review opens:</p>
<ul>
<li>a data steward checks the permissions and any identifying details in the text and photos;</li>
<li>the clinical lead verifies rare-disease status and the reference diagnosis against the evidence;</li>
<li>a request for more information opens a new revision under the same ID, and the history is kept;</li>
<li>an accepted case follows the photo path in <a href="/docs/data/privacy">Data and privacy</a>. If annotation is permitted, it can enter annotation queues, even with an unknown diagnosis.</li>
</ul>
<p>Credit for your contribution follows your attribution choice; see <a href="/docs/contributing/credit">Contribution and credit</a>.</p>
