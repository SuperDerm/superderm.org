Getting Started

To annotate photos you need an account, the accepted community rules, membership of the annotation project and a passed practice task. Steps 1 to 3 happen once. Step 4 happens once per queue and guide version.

1. Sign In with an Email Link#

  1. Go to Sign in and enter your email address.
  2. Select Email Me a Sign-In Link.
  3. Open the email "Your SuperDerm sign-in link" and follow the link.
  4. On the Confirm Sign-In page, select Continue to SuperDerm.

What you will see: you return to the page you started from, or to My workspace.

  • There is no password. Signing in also verifies your email address.
  • A link works once and expires after 20 minutes. If it has expired, request a new one.
  • You can request up to 5 links for one address in 15 minutes.
  • A session lasts 30 days on that device. To sign out, open My Workspace at the top right and select Sign Out.

In a development or preview environment without outgoing email, the link appears in the development mailbox instead of your inbox. See Data and privacy.

2. Accept the Community Rules#

The first time you annotate or contribute, you are sent to Before You Contribute.

  1. Read the community rules and the contribution terms. Each shows its version.
  2. Enter a display name of at least 2 characters.
  3. Choose how you are credited: display name, full name, or "Anonymous contributor".
  4. Tick the acceptance box and select Accept and Continue.

The platform records the exact versions you accepted. The current text is labelled as a draft under review. In short, the rules ask you to:

  • share only photographs you have the right to share, for the uses you state;
  • leave out names, record numbers, faces, tattoos and other identifiers, and report any you see;
  • describe what is visible, because a diagnosis suggested from photographs alone is a hypothesis;
  • keep independent assessments independent;
  • remember that SuperDerm gives no medical advice.

You can change your credit choice later. See Contribution and credit.

3. Join the Annotation Project#

On Annotate photos, select Join the Annotation Project. Membership records the project's participation terms.

4. Pass the Practice Task#

Each queue card lists what you still need under Requires. On the queue you want, select Take the Practice Task. Practice photographs are synthetic.

Queue type What you do To pass
Visible findings Outline the single raised finding with a box or polygon, then choose the terms that describe it Your outline covers the finding closely (an overlap of at least 0.5, measured as intersection over union) and at least one chosen term fits
Image quality Rate two photographs and select reasons where needed Every photograph is rated correctly, with a reason where one is required

If you do not pass, the page says what to change. Try again as often as you like. A pass is kept for the project, task type and guide version, and appears under Qualifications in your workspace. When a queue moves to a new guide version, take its practice task again.

Diagnosis verification has no practice task. That queue type is in preparation (Planned).

5. Claim a Photo#

  1. On a queue card, select Start Annotating.
  2. The platform claims one eligible task for you in a single step and opens the annotation workspace.

What you will see:

  • The claim is yours for 30 minutes and renews while your work is saved. See Using the workspace.
  • If you already hold an open claim in that queue, you get it back instead of a second one. The button then reads Resume or Claim, and the card lists your drafts.
  • You are never offered a task you have already worked on, or a case you contributed.
  • If nothing is left to claim, you see "Nothing left to claim in this queue right now." Try another task type or check back later.
  • A queue marked Requires verified clinical contributor needs that role. See Roles and permissions.

If no queue is open, the page shows "No photos are available for annotation yet". Photographs enter queues only after their permissions and privacy review are complete.

Your Workspace#

My workspace shows:

  • notifications, such as receipts, review decisions and release inclusion;
  • your annotations and drafts, with the next action: Resume, Respond to review, View Draft or View Result;
  • releases that include your work, with your credit;
  • your contributions, with their tracking IDs, status and a Resume Draft link for unsubmitted drafts;
  • your profile and credit settings, and your practice results.
