Annotation Guide

This guide explains what to mark and how to record it. For the controls, see Using the workspace. Each queue also has its own instructions and guide version, shown on the queue card and in the workspace.

Before You Start#

  • Describe only what the photographs show. Do not share your answers, or look up other people's answers for the same item.
  • SuperDerm uses clinical photographs from a phone or camera. If a photo is not a clinical photograph, for example a dermoscopic image or a micrograph, select Not a clinical photograph in an image quality task, or give that reason under Unable to assess these photos in a visible findings task.
  • If you see a face, a tattoo, a name, legible text or any other identifying detail, select Report Privacy Concern. The photo is paused immediately.
  • A description of visible findings is not a diagnosis.

Task Types#

Task Unit What you record
Image quality One photograph Whether the photo can be assessed, and why not
Visible findings All photos of one case Regions around visible findings, with descriptive terms
Diagnosis verification One case In preparation (Planned). It will require verified clinical credentials

Image Quality#

Choose one rating:

Rating Use it when
Assessable The finding of interest is visible clearly enough to describe.
Partially assessable Some of the finding can be described, but part is obscured, out of focus or out of frame.
Unable to assess The photograph cannot be assessed.

Then select any reasons that apply: Obstruction, Poor focus, Inadequate lighting, Not a clinical photograph. You can add a short note.

Unable to assess needs a reason: select a flag or write a note. It records the photo's quality only. It never means that no lesion is present.

Visible Findings#

Mark each visible finding as a separate region.

  1. Choose Box or Polygon and outline the finding on the photo where you see it. Each region belongs to exactly one photo.
  2. Choose one or more terms that describe the finding.
  3. Set Certain or Uncertain.
  4. Optionally add a note. Notes are stored with your annotation and are not published.

Box or Polygon#

  • Use a box for a compact finding. It is quickest to draw and to check.
  • Use a polygon when the outline is irregular, or when a box would take in a lot of unaffected skin.
  • Outline the whole finding and no more.

The platform checks every shape before you can submit:

  • A box must have positive width and height.
  • A polygon needs at least three distinct vertices, no repeated consecutive vertices, a positive area and no crossing edges.
  • Every point must lie inside the photo.

Terms#

Choose terms from the morphology vocabulary, up to 12 per region. Filter the list by name; each term shows a short definition.

Group Terms
Primary lesions Macule, Patch, Papule, Plaque, Nodule, Vesicle, Bulla, Pustule, Wheal, Cyst
Secondary changes Scale, Crust, Erosion, Ulcer, Fissure, Excoriation, Lichenification, Atrophy, Scar, Eschar or necrosis
Vascular and purpuric Petechiae, Purpura, Telangiectasia
Arrangement and shape Annular, Grouped or clustered, Linear, Umbilicated

This vocabulary is a DRAFT (version 0.1.0-draft). It has not been clinically approved. The clinical lead and vocabulary maintainers must approve its names, definitions and size conventions before a real annotation batch opens. The workspace labels it "Vocabulary 0.1.0-draft (draft)". Size cut-offs, such as macule versus patch, vary between references and often cannot be measured in a photograph. The approved guide will say how to handle unknown size.

Term Not Listed. If no term fits, describe the finding in a few words under Term Not Listed. Every region needs at least one term or a "Term not listed" description. Reviewers see these descriptions. They are excluded from public release packages.

Certain or Uncertain#

Every region starts as Certain. Choose Uncertain when you are not sure that the region shows the finding, or that the chosen terms fit. The reviewer sees your choice, and it is kept in releases.

Finding Without Location#

Select Finding Without Location when you can describe a finding but cannot mark where it is on the photo. The region is recorded as Cannot localize. It still needs a term or a "Term not listed" description.

Unable to Assess These Photos#

If none of the photos can be assessed, tick Unable to assess these photos and explain why. Remove any findings first; the two cannot be combined. Like the image quality rating, it records only that the photos could not be assessed. It never means that no lesion is present.

Independent Assessment#

During an independent assessment you see only the photographs, the instructions and your own annotations. Model predictions, other people's annotations and any answer supplied with the task are hidden.

Some queues collect several independent assessments of each case; the queue card shows how many. Each assessment is reviewed on its own.

An assisted mode, which would show model suggestions and record the model, its version and your edits, is Planned.

After You Submit#

An independent clinical reviewer checks your work. You see the outcome in your workspace. See How review works.
