How Review Works

Every annotation is checked by an independent clinical reviewer before it can enter a release. This page explains what reviewers decide and what you see as a contributor.

Who Reviews#

  • Reviewers hold the clinical reviewer role for the project, appointed by the clinical lead. See Roles and permissions.
  • A reviewer never sees a task they claimed, annotated or skipped, or a case they contributed. Contributors never review their own work.
  • Reviewers see each assessment labelled "Assessor 1", "Assessor 2" and so on, not by name, so they judge the work rather than the person.

When a Task Reaches Review#

A task enters the review queue when every independent assessment it needs has been submitted. The queue card on Annotate photos shows how many independent submissions each item needs. Each assessment is decided on its own.

The reviewer sees the photographs with the assessment drawn on them, the terms, "Term not listed" descriptions, Certain or Uncertain, and any earlier decisions on the task.

Decisions#

Decision Meaning Feedback to the contributor
Approve The annotation is accurate and follows the guide. Optional
Approve with edits The reviewer corrects a copy: moves shapes or vertices, removes findings, or changes terms and certainty. Optional
Return for more information The contributor revises the annotation and resubmits it. Required
Insufficient evidence The photos do not support a decision. Required

An approved result, with or without edits, can enter the next release if its photographs are cleared for redistribution. Returned and insufficient-evidence assessments never enter a release.

Feedback and Internal Notes#

Reviewers write two kinds of text:

  • Feedback to the contributor is sent to you with the decision and shown in your workspace and in the task.
  • Internal Note is for reviewers and leads only. It is never shown to the contributor.

What You See as a Contributor#

  • A notification "Review decision for task …" naming the decision, with the reviewer's feedback.
  • In My workspace, the review status of each task and the start of the feedback.
  • If your work was returned, select Respond to review. The task opens with Reviewer feedback at the top. Edit it and select Resubmit. Returned work has no claim timer.

The Original Is Always Kept#

  • Approve with Edits stores the reviewer's corrected copy as the accepted result, marked "approved with edits". Your submitted revision is not changed.
  • A resubmission creates a new revision. The earlier revision is marked superseded, not deleted, and its feedback stays on record.
  • Every decision is recorded with the reviewer, time, decision, feedback and internal note, and appears in the decision history that reviewers see.
  • Each revision gets one decision. A second decision on the same revision is refused.

Task States#

State Meaning
Open No one has claimed the task yet.
Collecting At least one slot is claimed; not every independent submission is in.
Ready for review Every submission is in.
In review Some assessments are decided, others are pending.
Accepted Every assessment was approved, with or without edits.
Returned At least one assessment was returned to its contributor.
Insufficient evidence Every assessment is decided, none is returned, and at least one was recorded as insufficient evidence.
Suspended A photo lost its annotation permission when the batch was reopened.

Each slot of a task is claimed, then submitted. Skipping a task or letting a claim expire releases the slot for someone else.

Your own assessment has its own review status: pending, approved, approved with edits, returned, insufficient evidence, or superseded.

Diagnosis Verification#

Diagnosis verification is Planned. Two people will make independent assessments of a case, and a credentialed clinical reviewer who took part in neither will adjudicate, using the authorized source evidence. A diagnosis suggested from photographs alone remains a hypothesis, even when assessors agree.
